# Laboratory staff

An investigation is requested, a sample is taken, a result is reported. The software
tracks all three, and the middle one is where mistakes hide.

## What you see when you log in

**Laboratory.** Investigations requested and reported.

## Your first hour

1. Open **Patients** and find a patient with an investigation requested.
2. Note the investigation number the hospital assigned, and the **accession** number
   your laboratory assigns. They are different, and they must both be right.
3. Enter a result against a test investigation.

## Your first week

- Learn that two specimens cannot share an accession. The software refuses a
  duplicate, because a shared accession is a mix-up, not a duplicate.
- Learn that a result carries who verified it. A result nobody verified is not a
  result.
- Learn the worklist: investigations by status, so you can see what is outstanding
  without asking anyone.

## What you must never do

- **Never enter a result you did not verify.** Your name is on it.
- **Never reuse an accession to save time.** The refusal is the feature.
- **Never report a result against the wrong investigation** to "fix" a mistake. Void
  it and request it again; a corrected result that overwrites the first hides the fact
  that a patient was told something wrong.

## What the software will not let you do

You cannot prescribe, dispense or bill. You cannot edit a result once verified without
it being recorded as a correction.
