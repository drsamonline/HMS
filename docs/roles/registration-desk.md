# Registration desk

You are the first person a patient meets and the last person they blame. Most of what
goes wrong in a hospital's records starts at this desk, and most of it is a
misspelling.

## What you see when you log in

**Front Desk.** Two things, because you need two things: register a patient, and see
who is waiting.

## Your first hour

1. Open **Patients** and search for a name you know is not there. The empty result is
   what you will see most often, and it should not look like a failure.
2. Register one test patient with a name you will recognise. Do not use a real
   patient's details.
3. Find that patient again by number, by name and by phone. All three should work.
4. Open the **OPD Queue** and see your test patient in it.
5. Delete nothing. There is no delete; ask an administrator if something is genuinely
   wrong.

## Your first week

- Learn the numbering. Patient numbers come from a sequence the hospital configured
  during setup. They do not repeat and they do not skip. If one does, stop and tell
  the administrator - it is not a display problem.
- Learn what a **visit** is versus a **patient**. One patient, many visits, forever.
  Registering the same person twice is the single most common error at this desk.
- Learn to search before you register. Search by phone number first; it is the thing
  people remember and the thing that is hardest to misspell twice.

## What you must never do

- **Never register a patient "just to see".** Every registration consumes a number and
  appears in the count a medical superintendent reports.
- **Never guess a spelling to save time.** A record with the wrong name is worse than
  no record, because it looks correct.
- **Never share your login.** Everything you do is attributed to you by name, and the
  trail is what the hospital relies on when a record is questioned.

## What the software will not let you do

You cannot open clinical records, prescriptions, bills or reports. You cannot delete a
patient. You cannot change a patient number. Those are not locked to inconvenience
you: a registration clerk who can edit a clinical record is an audit finding.
