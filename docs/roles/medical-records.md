# Medical records

You keep the hospital's memory. When an assessor asks for a record from three years
ago, you are the person who either finds it or explains why it is not there.

## What you see when you log in

**Medical Records.** Files, archives and certificates.

## Your first hour

1. Open **Patients** and find a patient's file location.
2. Open the certificate register and see what has been issued, with its retention date.
3. Open **Historical Data** and see which years have been archived.

## Your first week

- Learn the retention tiers. Live, online archive, cold archive - each has a rule about
  how quickly it must be reachable, and the rules are in
  `docs/ARCHIVE_RETENTION_TIERS.md`.
- Learn that a certificate is kept for at least three years and that the date is fixed
  when it is issued. A settings change cannot shorten an obligation already made.
- Learn what a referral needs: a written case statement. The software refuses a
  referral without one.

## What you must never do

- **Never delete a record because it is past its retention date.** Three years is a
  floor, not a ceiling. Past retention means archivable, never removable.
- **Never move a file without recording where it went.** A file nobody can find is a
  file that does not exist.
- **Never issue a certificate copy without checking the original is still held.**

## What the software will not let you do

You cannot prescribe or bill. You cannot delete a certificate. You cannot shorten a
retention date.
