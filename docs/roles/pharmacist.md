# Pharmacist

You dispense what the doctor prescribed and you keep the shelf honest. Both halves are
in the software, and the second half is the one that gets skipped.

## What you see when you log in

**Pharmacy.** Dispensing and stock.

## Your first hour

1. Open **Inventory** and find one medicine you know is on the shelf.
2. Dispense a test prescription against a test patient. Watch the stock quantity move.
3. Open **Procurement** and see what is on order.

## Your first week

- Learn the difference between a stock movement and a stock correction. One is a
  normal day; the other is a decision that leaves a trail.
- Learn what happens when stock goes to zero. It should stop you dispensing, not warn
  you and let you continue.
- Learn the expiry view. Expired stock on a shelf is the finding an inspector writes
  first.

## What you must never do

- **Never correct stock by editing a number to match the shelf.** That erases the
  reason the two disagreed, which is the only useful information in the discrepancy.
- **Never dispense against a prescription you cannot see.** If it is not in the
  system, the patient does not have one.
- **Never substitute a medicine without recording it.** A substitution nobody wrote
  down is indistinguishable from a dispensing error later.

## What the software will not let you do

You cannot prescribe, you cannot change a prescription, and you cannot open clinical
notes beyond what the prescription shows. You cannot delete a stock movement.
