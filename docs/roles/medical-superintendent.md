# Medical superintendent

You are accountable for the whole hospital and you have less time than anyone in it.
The software gives you one screen and one number per question.

## What you see when you log in

**Hospital Overview.** Statistics, inpatient activity, and the audit trail.

## Your first hour

1. Open **Statistics** and read every indicator with its definition. If a definition
   surprises you, that is the most useful hour you will spend this month.
2. Note which indicators say **no data**. Those are the questions your hospital cannot
   currently answer.
3. Open **Audit / Activity** and look at what happened yesterday.

## Your first week

- Read `docs/NABH-PSQ3.md`. It says which of the PSQ.3 families this hospital can
  report, which it cannot, and what each gap would cost to close. It is deliberately
  harsh, and it is the document to take to an assessor rather than a dashboard of
  numbers nobody can explain.
- Read `docs/NABH-MAPPING.md` and note the rows marked `absent`. Those are your
  roadmap, not your marketing.
- Decide who your auditor is. A hospital that can produce evidence nobody has
  independently reviewed has evidence, not assurance.

## What you must never do

- **Never quote an indicator without its definition.** "Our occupancy is 87%" is a
  claim; "87% of 120 active beds were occupied at month end" is a statement.
- **Never let a dashboard number stand in for a clinical judgement.** The software
  counts what was recorded, which is not the same as what happened.
- **Never ask for a record to be changed to make a report look better.** The audit
  trail will record the change and the reason, and the reason is the part that gets
  read.

## What the software will not let you do

Almost nothing is hidden from you, and almost everything you do is recorded. That
asymmetry is deliberate: oversight is a role with a trail, not a role without one.
