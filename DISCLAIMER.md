## What HHMS is

HHMS is **record-keeping and hospital administration software**. It stores what clinicians enter, keeps the ledger straight, and prints the documents a hospital needs.

It is **not a medical device**. It does not diagnose, does not suggest a remedy, does not calculate a dose, does not interpret a laboratory result and does not recommend a treatment. It has no clinical decision support function.

## Clinical responsibility

Every clinical decision — the case-taking, the remedy, the potency, the dose, the follow-up interval, the admission and the discharge — is made by the registered practitioner treating the patient, and remains theirs. HHMS records that decision; it does not make it.

Where HHMS displays a value that a clinician might act on — a derived invoice balance, a stock count, an investigation result entered by hand — the clinician remains responsible for checking it against the primary record before acting. Software transcribes; it does not verify.

## Data entry

The accuracy of a patient record is the responsibility of the person who enters it. HHMS validates format and relationships, not clinical truth. A wrong date of birth, a wrong blood group or a wrong prescription entry will be stored and printed faithfully.

## Records and the law

HHMS helps a hospital keep records. It does not, by itself, make the hospital compliant with any record-keeping obligation that applies to it, whether under the National Commission for Homoeopathy framework, state medical records rules, or the Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023. Those obligations attach to the hospital.

## Availability

HHMS runs on the hospital's own network and hardware. Its availability is the availability of that hardware. The Licensor does not operate the server and cannot restore it remotely. Backups, their testing and their off-site copies are the hospital's responsibility.
